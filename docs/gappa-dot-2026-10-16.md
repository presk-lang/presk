# A contraction's bound proved in Gappa

D108 proved the bound of one nest, the layer norm: elementwise operations and one sum of D terms. The shape that dominates a network is a contraction, a sum of products, which the native backend sums in the realization's declared order: sequential, or `interleaved(n)`, n partial sums added at the end. This run proves one, at the lengths of a network's products.

## What ran

**The program.** `experiments/gappa_dot.psk`'s `dot`: the product of an 8 × K and a K × 8 matrix, f32, synthesized from seed 0.
- The two operands go through a site each, `x = a + 0` and `w = b + 0`, which is exact in every type. The envelope then records their magnitudes, which an entry's arguments are not (D108).
- Its `dot` kernel is the bare product's: the script compares the two dumps at every width and order.

**The widths and orders.**
- K = 8, 16, 32, 64 and 128 (the course GPT's head dimension is 64; D is 384).
- Each width under the sequential order. The default kernel is register-blocked over eight output columns, each column summed in index order, bit for bit sequential (D32).
- Each width under `interleaved(n)` for n = 4, 8 and 16, where n divides K and leaves at least two terms to each partial sum.

**At each width and order:**
- `presk calibrate --precision` on the entry at 128 bits;
- `presk check --model all --measure` under the realization: the four rules and the gap of the kernels to the exact reference, on the envelope's own inputs;
- `presk dump --stage kernel`.

**The Gappa files** are written by `experiments/gappa_dot.py`, a second generator in the style of D108's.
- *The element:* one output element, y₀₀, with one rounding per operation the kernel performs, in its order.
- *The operations:* each operand rounded once into `f32` at the seam, each product rounded, then each partial sum.
  - Under `interleaved(n)`: the n accumulators over the terms k ≡ t (mod n), then the partials added left to right, as the kernel does.
- *The exact value:* the same tree over the reals.
- *The hypotheses:*
  - `operands`: every element of x's row and w's column bounded by its site's largest magnitude in the envelope;
  - `sites`: the same, and the output's largest magnitude.

  The envelope records no partial sum.
- *The hint:* one, as D108's: every accumulator's first addition, 0 + P, is exact.
- *The procedure:* `gappa.sh`'s. A query (`|Y − y| in ?`), then the same file with the bound found as its goal, a proof Gappa checks (exit 0), with the change threshold at 10⁻⁴.

**Gappa.**
- *Why built from source:* falicon has no `gappa` package installed; D108 ran on an x86-64 machine with Debian's 1.6.0.
- *The build:* 1.6.0, the same version, from its source release (`gappa-1.6.0.tar.gz`, sha256 `68d86dd1…3677dc483`), in `.gappa/` (git-ignored).
  - Configured with Boost 1.83's headers (`boost_1_83_0.tar.bz2`, sha256 `6478edfe…798c3b8e`, only `boost/` kept).
  - GMP and MPFR are the ones `gmp-mpfr-sys` builds for presk.
  - Built by `./remake`.
- *The check against D108:* on the layer-norm files D108 kept (`docs/gappa/ln_D8_sites.g`, `ln_D8_input.g`, `ln_D16_sites.g`), it gives D108's largest bound to the bit. One non-largest goal of `ln_D8_sites` comes out a hair tighter at its tenth significant digit, the same 9.16066·10⁻⁵ to six.

## What it says

**Gappa's bound stays within a small factor of the compiler's `cond` and does not open up with the sum's length.**
- *The sequential order:* proved ÷ `cond` is 2.59, 2.94, 2.90, 2.85 and 3.14 from K = 8 to 128, flat over a sixteenfold length. D108's layer norm gave 2.5–4.8×.
- *Interleaved:* the factor is 3.3 to 6.0×, rising with K.

**Both bounds grow as K², the measured gap much more slowly.**
- *Sequential:* each doubling of K multiplies the proved bound by 3.2 to 3.9 and `cond` by 2.8 to 3.6, while the measured gap grows ninefold in all from K = 8 to 128 (×1.7 a doubling, noisily). So `cond` ÷ measured rises from 13× to 164×, and proved ÷ measured from 32× to 514×.
- *Why both bounds:* a sequential sum's j-th partial sum is j terms large and rounds at u times that, so the worst case adds up to K²/2 terms' worth.
- *Why the gap:* the roundings of these random terms partly cancel, which is what `rss` estimates; `rss` is 2.5 to 15× the gap here.
- *Why they move together:* `cond`, (K − 1)·u·Σ|xᵢwᵢ|, reads the same worst case through the measured sum of the terms' magnitudes, itself linear in K; Gappa reads it through the operands' boxes. Both are quadratic in K.

**Interleaving lowers both bounds, Gappa's less than `cond`'s.**
- *`cond`:* charges `interleaved(n)` k/n + n roundings against k − 1, so at K = 128 it is 5.1× lower under `interleaved(8)` than sequential. 127 against 24, and `interleaved(16)`'s 24 too; the table's equal `cond`s at K = 32, 64 and 128 for two orders are this count's coincidences.
- *Gappa:* its proof at K = 128 drops 2.8×.
- *The ratio:* proved ÷ `cond` rises from 3 to about 5.
- *Not decided here:* whether that is slack in Gappa's interval reasoning on the n partial sums added at the end, or `cond`'s count being tight. Both are sound; neither proof is examined beyond its bound.

**The measured gap.**
- *Under interleaving:* it is smaller than sequential's at K ≥ 64 (2.4–3.4·10⁻⁶ against 5.2 and 5.0·10⁻⁶).
- *At K = 16:* `interleaved(4)` and sequential give the same largest gap, 1.3920·10⁻⁶. A numpy recomputation of both orders on the same inputs gives the same: at that element, and at one other, the two orders round to the same `f32` sum.

## Found and left

- **The envelope records no partial sum**, so Gappa's hypotheses are the operands' boxes and the output's magnitude. The compiler's `cond` reads the measured Σ|xᵢwᵢ|, which no hypothesis of the Gappa file states. A tighter proof would need the partial sums' magnitudes, which `presk calibrate` does not keep. `sites` (adding |y|) tightens the proof only 1.1 to 1.9×.
- **Gappa's time grows about sevenfold per doubling of K** under the sequential order (8 s, 58 s, 423 s at 32, 64, 128). K = 384, the course GPT's D, would take hours by that trend; it was not run.
- **A second generator.** `gappa_dot.py` mirrors the kernel's order as `gappa_layernorm.py` does, by hand. There is still no Gappa export from the compiler (D108).
