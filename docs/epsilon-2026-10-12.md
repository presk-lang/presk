# ε from the margins: the largest budget on the logits that guarantees the accuracy, on the course GPT (D118)

2026-10-12, presk `main` `c73a2e1` (the tag `v0.11.0`, 0.11.0's code),
`make margins` (`experiments/margins.sh`, `experiments/margins.py`,
`experiments/margins.psk`), on falicon. The prompt is
`presk-project/docs/prompt-epsilon-margins-2026-10-12.md`, the argument
`presk-project/docs/presk-epsilon-from-quality-2026-10-01.md` §1. Nothing
here is timed; the compiler is unchanged. The files of record are
archived as `presk-project/data/epsilon-0.11.0.tar.xz`: the 118 016
margins, every realization's flips and decisions, the report's tables and
their JSON.

## 0. The decisions, before anything ran

**The statement, S**, fixed before the margins were taken, in the prompt's
words (the letter given on 2026-10-13, for the paper's Table 2): *at
δ = 10⁻³ and α = 0.05 on the 461 held-out windows, every
realization of the curve whose measured gap on the logits is at most ε\*
flips at most δ of the exact model's decisions, and the number of flips of
each is at most the number of positions whose margin is below twice its
measured gap.* It holds or fails as measured.

One remark was written down with it, before any result. On the sample the
margins were taken from, the first clause follows from the second: a
realization within ε\* has #{m < 2ε_meas} ≤ #{m < 2ε\*} ≤ k ≤ δn by the
construction of ε\*. So on its own sample the statement tests the
argument and the measurement, the deterministic part, and not the
statistical part. That part is the population claim Pr[m < 2ε\*] ≤ δ. It
is tested out of sample here, beside the statement and outside its letter:
ε\* taken from the even windows and the margins counted on the odd ones,
and the reverse.

**The model and the data** are `make foil`'s, so that the foil's table
and this one read the same runs:
- the course GPT at D = 64, H = 2, L = 2, T = 256;
- the weights `out/curve_train_f64/final.psk.d`, `make curve`'s all-`f64`
  run, 600 steps on 90 % of the Code civil (D117's, byte-identical to
  D102's);
- the inputs `out/civil_val_T256.psk.d`, the other 10 %: 461 windows,
  118 016 positions, every one held out.

`--seed 0` is passed to every run. Outside a training run `dropout`'s mask
is all ones, the expectation of the random function (the manual's
`training` chapter), so the key changes no value here. Every realization
sees the same inputs, and a flip is the realization's.

**The exact model** is the reference interpreter at 128 bits, the paper's
reference for every gap. It takes 3.8 s a window. The 461 windows ran
once, in chunks of 32 windows, eight chunks at a time, each chunk 2 min
and 1.4 GB, so the native `f64` fallback was not needed. Its logits are
printed at 30 digits and kept outside the repository (`out/margins/exact/`,
each logit as two doubles whose sum it is).

**The margins** are not an entry of the program. The margin needs a
maximum over the vocabulary, and `max` is not implemented (E2012). The
prompt's form `max(axis vocab, logits − c · one_hot(argmax(logits)))`
needs it too. So `experiments/margins.py` takes the margin from the
printed exact logits, as the top one minus the largest of the others. It
computes in decimal at 60 digits from the 30 printed, so the margin is
exact to about 10⁻³⁰. The realization's decisions are an entry,
`decisions`, which is `argmax` natively under the realization. The
reference defines `argmax` as the greatest index among equal maxima; the
interpreter and the kernels agree, and so does the script.

**δ and α**: δ ∈ {10⁻², 10⁻³, 10⁻⁴}, α = 0.05.
- **Uncorrected,** ε\*(δ) is half the (k + 1)-th smallest margin with
  k = ⌊δn⌋, the largest ε with at most k margins below 2ε. With n = 118 016,
  k is 1 180, 118 and 11; at 10⁻⁴ that is the twelfth smallest margin.
- **Corrected,** k is the largest count whose one-sided Clopper–Pearson
  bound is at most δ, that is P[Bin(n, δ) ≤ k] ≤ α. This gives k = 1 123,
  99 and 5. At 10⁻⁴ the correction moves ε\* from the twelfth margin to
  the sixth.

**The realizations**, fourteen:
- **The foil's seven,** made as `experiments/foil.sh` makes them: `f64`,
  `f32`, `bf16`, and `int8` and `int4` weights on every matrix with `f32`
  elsewhere, each in `absmax` and in `pow2`.
- **The curve's seven,** D117's `out/curve_1e-1.json` … `curve_1e-7.json`,
  statement D's among them at 10⁻³. They were solved on `loss` over
  `make gpt-trained`'s weights and are applied here to the held-out
  weights, as the foil applies statement D's. Their sites are
  `GPT.apply/…`, `Block.apply/…` and so on, which `experiments/margins.psk`
  calls under the same names. Their `loss/…` sites name nothing here.

**The sample for the gap on the logits** is the whole validation set:
the exact logits are kept once and each realization's native logits
compared with them. `presk quantize --entry logits` gives the same figure,
the largest elementwise gap of a tensor entry (it printed 1.958·10⁻⁴ on
the first window at 10⁻³, as the script does). But it runs the exact
reference again on each call, 14 × 461 windows. Beside each row is the
foil's own measure: the gap to the exact loss on two held-out windows,
relative, by `presk quantize --entry loss`.

**Step 4 was checked first:** `presk solve` does not take a
tensor-valued entry. `presk solve experiments/margins.psk … ` on `logits`
answers "`logits` must return a scalar `ℝ[]`, since the sites are ranked
by its sensitivity `∂L/∂v`". So no realization is solved at ε\*.

**The decision number**: D118, free on `origin`'s branches and with no
open merge request (the merge request list, 2026-10-12). Plan 0.12.0
(presk-specs `implementation/presk-plan-0.12.0.md`, step 0) names D118 for
the decision that opens it; it will take the next free number.

**Found at step 0, and left** (no compiler change here):
- **`presk run --json` misprints its inputs under `--weights`.** The
  result is right, but the `inputs` field prints the arguments the
  command synthesized: the initial parameters and a random batch, not the
  checkpoint's parameters nor the batch `--data` gave. On the first window
  its tokens are not the data file's, and its targets are not its inputs
  shifted by one. The argmax of the printed logits scores 0.2266 against
  the data's targets, as the `accuracy` entry does, and 0.0117 against the
  printed ones. The cause is `presk-cli/src/main.rs`, `run`'s `--weights`
  branch: `presk_train::args_on_data` returns the synthesized `inputs`
  beside the arguments it actually passes. The script takes the targets
  from `presk data export` instead. (Fixed on 2026-10-13, after this
  report: `--data` alone printed a synthesized batch too, and both paths

- **D53's foil, re-run at 0.11.0** (`make foil`, D117's statement D
  realization), equals D102's table at the tag `v0.10.0` in every column

**What the table checks:**
- **Each flip, position by position:** every flip of every row is at a
  position whose margin is at most twice that position's own gap. The
  argument is checked at each of the 7 371 flips, not only in count.
- **The decisions against the logits:** `decisions`' choices equal the
  argmax of the same realization's printed logits at every position of
  every row, chunks of 32 windows against one run of 461.
- **The accuracy against the foil's column:** every accuracy equals the
  foil's for the same realization.

**Reading it:**
- **Every realization's flips are within its bound**, all fourteen.
  - *Bound over flips:* 28.5 (`bf16`) to 249 (`int8 pow2`), and 70 to
    100 on the curve's rows. A flip needs the error to point the wrong
    way at a close call, and most errors near a close call do not.
  - *Where the slack comes from:* taking the largest gap over all the
    positions costs a factor of 2 (`int4 pow2`) to 23 (`int8`), and about
    12 on the curve's rows. The per-position bound is then within 9–12×
    of the flips for `bf16` and the two `int8` rows, and 15–17× for the
    two `int4` rows.
- **Statement D's realization, solved at 10⁻³ on the loss, is certified
  at 10⁻³.** It measures 3.35·10⁻⁴ on the logits, under ε\*(10⁻³) =
  4.67·10⁻⁴, so its margins allow 70 flips. It flips one decision, at the
  smallest margin of the 118 016 (1.19·10⁻⁵, `' '` against `n`, the
  target being neither), where its gap at that position exceeds half the
  margin. The four tighter budgets are
  certified too, 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁷ at 10⁻⁴ as well, and flip nothing.
- **A budget on the loss does not bound the logits.** The curve's 10⁻¹
  and 10⁻² realizations are inside their budgets on the loss: 4.2·10⁻⁶
  and 1.2·10⁻⁵ here, relative. On the logits they are 3.2·10⁻² and
  1.4·10⁻² from the exact values, 68 and 30 times ε\*(10⁻³), and they
  flip 69 and 36 decisions. On the foil's two windows at 10⁻³, the
  logits are 1.96·10⁻⁴ from the exact values and the loss 7.0·10⁻⁷
  (absolute), 280 times less. The loss is a mean over 512 positions, and
  it averages the errors that each decision sees alone. Both rows flip
  fewer than δn = 118 decisions, but the margins cannot show it: the
  certificate needs the gap where the decisions are taken.
- **The flips follow the logits' gap, not the loss's.** In the logits'
  gap order, `bf16` and the two `int8` rows are out of order (480 flips
  at 5.0·10⁻², 161 and 168 at 7.8·10⁻² and 1.15·10⁻¹). Their typical
  position orders them: `bf16`'s median per-position gap is 1.7·10⁻²,
  `int8`'s 5.9·10⁻³. The per-position bound (4 916, 1 488, 2 061) orders
  the three as the flips do. In the loss's gap, `int8` (3.9·10⁻⁵) is 44
  times closer than `bf16` (1.7·10⁻³), and flips a third as many
  decisions.

## 4. A solve at ε\*

`presk solve` takes a scalar entry only. It ranks the sites by the
entry's sensitivity `∂L/∂v`, and on `logits` it stops at once: "`logits`
must return a scalar `ℝ[]`". A budget on the logits would need a
sensitivity per output component, or a scalar program whose gap bounds
the logits' largest. `max` would write the latter, and it is not
implemented. The verification already exists: `presk quantize --entry
logits` measures the ∞-norm gap of a tensor entry. That is a note for
0.12.0 or later, not this step, and no realization was solved at ε\*.

## 5. The foil, re-read

MLPerf's rule admits all eight of the foil's rows at 99 %. At 99.9 % it
admits seven and refuses `int4`, absmax, which loses 136 correct
predictions.

The margins certify three of the eight at both 10⁻² and 10⁻³: `f64`,
`f32` and statement D's realization. They refuse `bf16`, both `int8`
rows and both `int4` rows at every δ measured.

The two rules disagree on four rows, `bf16`, `int8`, `int8 pow2` and
`int4 pow2`, which MLPerf admits at 99.9 % and the margins refuse:
- **At 10⁻³ the margins are right.** `bf16`, `int8` and `int8 pow2`
  change 480, 161 and 168 decisions. That is 4.1·10⁻³, 1.4·10⁻³ and
  1.4·10⁻³ of them, each more than δ. Net accuracy, the count MLPerf
  thresholds, hides it: −2, +6 and −11 correct, since a change of decision
  is as often a gain as a loss.
- **At 10⁻² they are conservative.** Those three change fewer than δ of
  the decisions, but their gaps on the logits are 9 to 21 times
  ε\*(10⁻²) = 5.4·10⁻³. A certificate from the largest gap of a weight
  rounding is a worst case. For `int8` the worst logit is 13 times the
  median position's, and its bound, 28.5 % of the positions, covers more
  than a quarter of the decisions.

The note's §5 expected `int8` to be "acceptable exactly when the margins
say so". They say so only at a δ above 28 %, so `int8` weights are not
certified by the margins on this model, though they change 0.14 % of its
decisions.

The two `int4` rows show the difference most sharply. MLPerf refuses one
and admits the other, at −136 and +115 correct. Yet they change 2 968 and
3 488 decisions, 2.5 % and 3.0 %, the admitted one more. Their gaps on
the logits (1.24 and 2.08) exceed every margin, so the bound covers every
position and the margins refuse both at any δ.

So the margins order the realizations by their gap on the logits, as the
budget does, and they bound what a realization changes rather than what
it scores. MLPerf's rule reads the net score, which is not monotone in any
gap: the foil's "it separates one realization and not by its gap" has its
counterpart here.

## 6. What the statement decides

**S holds.** At δ = 10⁻³ and α = 0.05, ε\* = 4.67·10⁻⁴.
- **Five of the curve's seven realizations are certified:** 10⁻³ to
  10⁻⁷, with gaps on the logits from 6.7·10⁻⁸ to 3.3·10⁻⁴. Each flips at
  most one decision, against δn = 118.
- **All seven curve rows flip at most their bound**, and so does every
  other row: 1 against 70, 36 against 3 090, 69 against 6 933.
- **Out of sample**, the fraction of margins below 2ε\* stays under δ on
  both halves at every δ.

As written at step 0, on its own sample the first clause follows from
the second. What the measurement adds is that the second holds at every
one of 7 371 flips, position by position, and that the quantile's
promise holds on data it was not taken from.
## 7. What it does not say

- **One model, one validation set, one seed, one length of training.**
  The course GPT at D = 64 after 600 steps, on the last tenth of the Code
  civil. A model trained longer is more confident and its margins larger;
  one that is not is the opposite. ε\* is a property of these weights on
  this data, as the envelope is (D35).
- **The margins are of the trained weights, not of the budget's
  envelope.** The curve's realizations were solved on `loss` over other
  weights and random tokens. Here they are applied to the held-out weights
  and measured on the logits, which the solver never bounded: a budget on
  the loss does not bound the logits (§3). The certificate is the measured
  gap's, on this sample, and not the budget's.
- **The argument bounds flips, not loss or perplexity.** A realization
  inside ε\* changes at most a fraction δ of the decisions; how much the
  loss moves is the Lipschitz route of the note's §2, not measured here.
- **The statistical part fails under distribution shift; the
  deterministic part does not.** On any input, a position whose margin is
  at least twice the realization's gap there keeps its decision. That
  Pr[m < 2ε\*] ≤ δ holds for text like the Code civil's last tenth, and
  for nothing else.
- **The binomial correction treats positions as independent draws.**
  They are 256 consecutive positions of 461 consecutive windows of one
  text: neighbours share their context. The split by windows (§1) is the
  check of what the correction promises. A correction by windows instead
  (461 draws) would be far wider: Hoeffding over 461 windows adds 0.057
  to the fraction, useless at δ = 10⁻³.
- **The gap of record is the largest over 10 857 472 logits.** It is a
  measurement on these inputs, like every gap in the paper, and not a
  bound on other inputs. A realization certified here is certified for
  the inputs whose gap it was measured on.

## 8. The commands and the record

```sh
make margins                    # experiments/margins.sh: 5 minutes on falicon, outputs reused
DELTAS="1e-2 1e-3 1e-4" ALPHA=0.05 CHUNK=32 JOBS=8 ./experiments/margins.sh
WINDOWS=2 CHUNK=1 OUT=out/margins-2 ./experiments/margins.sh   # the pipeline on two windows, in 30 s
```

It needs `make curve`'s outputs (the realizations and the all-`f64` run's
weights; `QUALITY=0` solves without training, and the all-`f64` run is
four minutes). It writes under `out/margins/`:
- `exact/`, the exact logits by chunk, 172 MB;
- `native/<realization>/`, per position the largest gap, the argmax and
  the sum of the gaps;
- `decisions/`;
- `margins.md`, the tables above, and `margins.json`, every figure;
- `record/margins.tsv`, the 118 016 margins with the exact top two and
  the target;
- `record/flips.tsv`, every realization's flips.

`docs/epsilon/` keeps `margins.md` and `margins.json`. The two record
files are 1.8 MB compressed, so they are archived as
`presk-project/data/epsilon-0.11.0.tar.xz` (1.8 MB, with its `.sha256`),
together with every realization's decisions, the foil's table at 0.11.0,
the realizations run and the logs, and a `SHA256SUMS`. The
exact logits are not archived, since they are retaken in 4 minutes.
