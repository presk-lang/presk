# δ_p: the reference at p bits against itself at more (W16)

Every budget of Presk is discharged by measurement against the reference interpreter (crate `presk-interp`, MPFR through `rug`, `--bits`, 256 by default; 128 in the `precision` chapter's calibrations), which computes the equations of the reference's chapter 3 in binary floating point with a significand of p bits, every operation rounded to the nearest, the sums term by term in rank order. The reference's chapter 4 (section "Discharge", definitions "Reference" and "Discharge by measurement") names its distance to the denotation δ_p and takes it as 0 against the reference at p ≥ 256 bits — a discharge conditional on δ_p lying below the resolution at which the measured gap is read, "which no bound of this reference establishes". This is the number that condition rests on.

**Method.** The denotation is not computable; the reference at a higher precision P stands in for it. When p rises to P every rounding of the run shrinks by 2^-(P−p), so δ_P ≪ δ_p and |f_p − f_P| ≈ δ_p, with |f_p − f_P| = δ_p ± δ_P. The estimate is checked by its own ratios: δ_p / δ_2p should be near 2^p (the roundings of one run are the only thing that moves between two precisions), and it is read in the form of each budget's gap — the ∞-norm relative to the output's largest magnitude for an entry (definition "Gap of an entry"), the 2-norm relative to the gradient's norm for the gradient (the gradient gap of definition "Gap of a training step").

**What is compared.** The course GPT (`corpus/nlp/05_gpt.psk`) at D = 64, H = 2, L = 2, T = 256 on `make gpt-trained`'s weights (600 steps natively in `f32` on this machine, 6 min 27 s, final training loss 2.73) and the first eight windows of `corpus/nlp/data/civil_T256.psk.d` (`--samples 8`, one batch of B = 8), the inputs the `precision` chapter's calibrations use. Each run is `presk run --json --digits 330` — MPFR's correctly rounded decimal at 330 significant digits, more than 1024 bits resolve (308) — and `experiments/delta_p.py` reads every leaf as a decimal number at 400 digits and compares exactly. Three entries, on a copy of the program with two entries appended (`out/delta_p/gpt_delta_p.psk`, written by the script):

1. `loss`, the program's own, the entry the forward budgets bind (D, D51, D93).
2. `logits`: `GPT.apply(θ, x, k)` with the batch as the last argument so that `--data` fills it — the model's `apply` in its inference reading, the entry a serve's budget binds (definition "What a budget binds", item 2; D53 measured BERT-large's logits so).
3. `loss_grad`: `grad(λ θ. loss(θ, k, batch))(θ)`, the gradient at the trained weights, the first half of a training step. `presk run` evaluates in the inference reading (every dropout mask is 1), so the gradient here is the loss's gradient without dropout, at the state θ_600 and not at D52's state (step 101 after a warm-up, with dropout drawn), which no command exposes at full precision: `presk calibrate --step` prints its gaps at four digits and writes no parameter (a checkpoint holds binary64 leaves). The step is therefore *checked* and not measured: its all-`f64` row at p = 128, 256, 512 and 1000 must read the same.

**What the method cannot see.** Anything computed in binary64 on the path is the same at every p, so a difference of two precisions does not show it. On these three entries nothing is: the weights are binary64 numbers read exactly (`presk_rt::load_params`, the data chapter), the literals denote binary64 numbers by the reference's own definition "What a literal denotes" (`real(1e-5)` is the binary64 nearest 10⁻⁵, injected exactly), `sqrt(HD : ℝ)` converts an integer exactly, the means divide by exact integers, and no draw is made. The function approximated is the same at every p, and it is the denotation. Two defects of the interpreter were found on the way, recorded in D98 and not fixed here (no compiler code changes in this merge request): the gradient at p ≥ 1016 bits never terminates (`prims.rs`, `machin_pi`: π for `gelu`'s derivative by Machin's series, whose stopping threshold 2^-(p+8) is computed in `f64` and is 0 from there), so the gradient's highest precision is 1000; and in a training step `dropout`'s scale 1/(1 − r) is computed in `f64` (`prims.rs`, `dropout`), 1.25 where chapter 3's equation gives 1/(1 − fl(0.2)) = 1.25 + 1.7·10⁻¹⁷ — a p-independent term of 1.4·10⁻¹⁷ relative on every kept activation that a comparison of precisions does not show and that is absent from these three entries.

## Reading

- **Five to ten units of roundoff at p bits.** 2⁻¹²⁸ = 2.9·10⁻³⁹, 2⁻²⁵⁶ = 8.6·10⁻⁷⁸, 2⁻⁵¹² = 7.5·10⁻¹⁵⁵: the loss is 10, 4.7 and 4.9 units off, the logits 8.3, 9.4 and 8.6, the gradient 10, 10 and 10. An entry of some 7·10⁸ elementary operations, a few thousand on the path to any one element, puts its result a handful of ulps from the exact one: the roundings compensate as a random walk's steps do and the sums are well conditioned (D51). δ_p is 2⁻ᵖ times ten, not 2⁻ᵖ times a worst case.
- **The estimate holds.** Every ratio δ_p / δ_2p is within a factor two of 2ᵖ (2¹²⁹, 2¹²⁸, 2¹²⁸ at 128 bits; 2²⁵⁶ three times at 256), so |f_p − f_2p| is δ_p and not δ_2p, and the run at 1024 bits stands in for the denotation to within 10⁻³⁰⁷ relative. The loss at 128 bits agrees with the loss at 1024 to 37 significant digits, at 256 to 76, at 512 to 153.
- **Against the measured gaps.** Every gap the compiler has measured was read at binary64's resolution, 2⁻⁵³ ≈ 1.1·10⁻¹⁶ relative, and the smallest are the `f64` loss's 1.3·10⁻¹⁵ (D93), the `f64` gradient's 9.9·10⁻¹⁶ (D93), BERT-large's `f64` logits 1.5·10⁻¹⁴ (D53) and the `f64` update 6.8·10⁻¹³ (D93), all against the reference at 128 bits. δ₁₂₈ ≈ 3·10⁻³⁸ is 21 orders of magnitude below the resolution and 22 below the smallest gap; δ₂₅₆ 60 and 61. On this model and these inputs, "δ_p taken as 0" is a measured statement; beyond them it is a claim of the envelope's kind (the reference's definition "Discharged on the sample, claimed on the domain").
- **The step.** The all-`f64` row does not move between 128 and 1000 bits at four digits: a δ_p of the exact step above 10⁻¹⁶ of the update would have shown. D93's figures (6.8·10⁻¹³, 4.8·10⁻¹⁴, 9.9·10⁻¹⁶, measured on falicon) come back on weights trained again on belvedere, so `make gpt-trained` and the native `f64` step give the same bits on x86-64 as on the Cortex cores.
- **The cost of p** (the loss, 8 windows, one thread): 47 s and 0.8 GB at 128 bits, 60 s and 1.0 GB at 256 (1.3×), 86 s and 1.4 GB at 512, 133 s and 2.2 GB at 1024 (2.8×, 2.7×). The gradient at 1000 bits, the longest run, 510 s and 8.9 GB. The interpreter's time is mostly its own, not MPFR's arithmetic, up to 1024 bits.

## Commands

```sh
make gpt-trained            # the weights (600 steps natively in f32, 6 min 27 s here)
make delta-p                # experiments/delta_p.sh, then experiments/delta_p.py
# one run, as the script issues it (loss at 1024 bits):
presk run out/delta_p/gpt_delta_p.psk --entry loss --const D=64 --const H=2 --const L=2 --dim T=256 \
    --weights out/gpt_trained/final.psk.d --data corpus/nlp/data/civil_T256.psk.d --samples 8 \
    --bits 1024 --digits 330 --json
# the step's check:
presk calibrate --step corpus/nlp/05_gpt.psk --const D=64 --const H=2 --const L=2 \
    --weights out/gpt_trained/final.psk.d --warmup 100 --steps 600 --samples 2 --from 4000 --bits 1000 --realize f64
```

The raw outputs (`out/delta_p/*.json`, 55 MB for the gradient at 1000 bits, and `*.time`) are not committed; `make delta-p` recomputes them in about forty minutes on one core, and a run whose output exists is not run again.
