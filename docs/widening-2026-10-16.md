# Where the measurement changes the solver's decision

Every solve of record was accepted as proposed: zero widening rounds, the estimate at 97 % of the budget and the gap 700 times inside it (D124). A reader may conclude that the measurement is a formality. This run looks for programs on which it is not: the solver's estimate admits a type, the measurement against the exact reference refuses it, and widening lands the realization on types that hold.

It found three, each a different thing a first-order estimate cannot see, and each beside a form of the same computation on which the estimate is right.

## What the estimate is

The solver proposes with **Σ s · u(type) · |v|** over the values the program stores (the precision chapter):
- s is the value's cotangent 1-norm, per output element, from one reverse sweep seeded with ones;
- u is the type's unit roundoff;
- |v| is the envelope's magnitude.

So it charges each stored value **one rounding, at its magnitude, to first order**. It then measures the proposal against the exact reference and, while the gap exceeds the budget, widens the narrowed value whose term of the estimate is largest, by one type.

`presk check --model all` prints a different family of quantities: the bounds `k`, `normwise` and `cond`, and the estimate `rss`. They propagate every rounding forward, a sum's included, `rss` assuming the roundings independent. Both families appear below.

## The programs

`experiments/widening.psk`, six entries, three pairs. Their inputs are synthesized standard normal from seed 0; the constants (`c`, `h`) are the entries' `f64` defaults.

| pair | the entry the estimate misjudges | the form beside it | rows |
|---|---|---|---|
| a value outside its type's range | `attn_raw`: one attention row, the weights an unshifted softmax of logits near 84, the output Σ p·v | `attn_softmax`: the same by the `softmax` primitive, whose kernel subtracts the row's maximum first | 8 |
| a sensitivity that vanishes where the error does not | `std_two_pass`: the standard deviation by the two-pass formula, rows offset to 1 000 | `std_one_pass`: the one-pass formula E[z²] − μ² | 64 |
| a sum's own roundings | `clock`: a sum of K = 4 096 equal terms, 0.1 each | `noisy`: the same terms plus 0.01 times the input | 4 |

The attention row returns Σ p·v rather than the weights p: the weights sum to 1 whatever the logits, and the seed of ones would then rank nothing (every sensitivity about 10⁻⁴⁰, the precision chapter's note on a tensor result). The standard deviation runs at 64 rows so that its row of smallest variance, whose error is the largest, is far from the mean row the estimate speaks of.

## Found and fixed first: an argument offered as a parameter

**The defect.** On these entries, before the fix, every solve widened, and every gap it printed was ∞.

- **The cause.** Since 0.12.0 the solver offers the entry's parameters by path, read off its first argument. When that argument is a tensor and not a parameter tree, it was offered anyway, as the parameter of path ``.
- **Why it widened.** No executor runs a non-parameter argument in a type of its own: `Realization::store_params` refuses it. So every measurement of a proposal that narrowed it failed and read as an infinite gap, and the solver widened every other value back before it reached that one.
  - On the one-pass formula at 8 rows and budget 10⁻¹: 6 rounds, every gap ∞, ending at all-`f64` but the constant.
  - With the fix: 0 rounds and 1.99×, measured 7.8·10⁻² against a budget of 1.1·10⁻¹.
- **The fix.** `param_sites` (`presk-driver/src/precision.rs`) offers nothing when the first argument is not a record.
- **The test.** `a_tensor_first_argument_is_no_parameter` (`presk-driver/tests/driver.rs`): the attention row by the primitive solves with no parameter offered and 0 rounds.
- **What it did not touch.** No solve of record had a tensor first argument: the course GPT, BERT and the decoder all take their parameters first.

Everything below is after the fix.

## The three cases

### A value outside its type's range: the unshifted softmax

Under one type everywhere (`presk check --model all --measure`):

| type | `k` | `normwise` | `cond` | `rss` | measured | `cond` ÷ measured | `rss` ÷ measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `attn_raw` `f64` | 3.56e-12 | 2.21e-12 | 3.40e-14 | 5.27e-15 | 1.44e-15 | 24× | 3.7× |
| `attn_raw` `f32` | 1.91e-3 | 1.19e-3 | 1.82e-5 | 2.83e-6 | **3.72e-1** | **4.9·10⁻⁵×** | **7.6·10⁻⁶×** |
| `attn_raw` `bf16` | 7.41e1 | 7.42e1 | 7.22e-1 | 1.47e-1 | 3.72e-1 | 1.9× | 0.4× |
| `attn_softmax` `f32` | 7.47e-4 | 7.02e-4 | 5.34e-5 | 1.63e-5 | 4.63e-7 | 115× | 35× |
| `attn_softmax` `bf16` | 4.58e1 | 4.58e1 | 3.27 | 1.04 | 8.33e-2 | 39× | 12× |

**What happens.**
- The largest row sum of `exp` is 3.777·10³⁸ in the envelope. `f32`'s largest finite value is 3.403·10³⁸, and `bf16` has the same exponent range.
- That row's sum overflows to ∞, its weights become 0, and its output 0: the measured gap is the output's whole magnitude, 0.372.
- **Every rule, the three bounds included, lies below the measured gap in `f32`**, by 4·10⁴ to 10⁵. They are first-order statements about small perturbations (the precision chapter says so), and a value past its type's range is not one. Nothing in them, nor in the estimate, compares the envelope's magnitude with the type's range.
- The primitive's kernel subtracts the row's maximum, nothing overflows, and every rule bounds the gap again (`cond` 115× above it in `f32`).

**The solves:**

| budget (absolute) | entry | proposal: estimate | proposal: measured | rounds, in order | final gap | cost vs all-`f64` | the culprit alone widened |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10⁻³ (3.7e-4) | `attn_raw` | 6.32e-6 | **3.72e-1** | 4: `e` (%5), `p·v` (%8), the output's sum (%9), the row's sum of `exp` (%6), each `f32`→`f64` | 1.44e-15 | 1.00× | 4.18e-7, 1.33× |
| 10⁻³ (3.7e-4) | `attn_softmax` | 5.53e-6 | 4.63e-7 | 0 | 4.63e-7 | 2.00× | — |
| 10⁻¹ (3.7e-2) | `attn_raw` | 2.09e-3 | 3.72e-1 | 6 (two of them `bf16`→`f32`) | 1.44e-15 | 1.00× | 9.79e-4, 1.34× |
| 10⁻¹ (3.7e-2) | `attn_softmax` | 1.46e-3 | 9.79e-4 | 0 | 9.79e-4 | 2.01× | — |

At every budget from 10⁻¹ to 10⁻⁶ the unshifted form widens (2 to 6 rounds) and ends at 1.00×; the shifted form never widens. "The culprit alone widened" is the proposal with only the row's sum and the nest that reads it in `f64`. That nest is `p·v`, into which `p = e / Σe` is fused, so it reads the sum in its own type. That realization holds the budget at 1.33×.

### A sensitivity that vanishes: the two-pass standard deviation

| type | `k` | `normwise` | `cond` | `rss` | measured | `cond` ÷ measured | `rss` ÷ measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `std_two_pass` `f32` | 1.34e-2 | 1.34e-2 | 1.16e-3 | 4.18e-5 | 6.11e-6 | 190× | 6.8× |
| `std_two_pass` `bf16` | 4.00e1 | 4.00e1 | 3.48 | 5.83e-1 | 8.55e-1 | 4.1× | **0.68×** |
| `std_one_pass` `f32` | 4.82 | 4.80 | 4.80 | 6.89e-1 | 3.05e-1 | 16× | 2.3× |
| `std_one_pass` `bf16` | 1.44e4 | 1.44e4 | 1.43e4 | 7.35e3 | ∞ (a negative variance, NaN) | — | — |

**What happens.**
- The two-pass result is stationary in the mean: Σ(z − μ) = 0, so the cotangent of μ is 4·10⁻⁴⁰, zero to the reference's digits. The estimate charges μ nothing in any type, and the proposal stores μ in `bf16`.
- At 1 000, `bf16`'s spacing is 4. The rounded μ moves every deviation z − μ by its whole error, and the standard deviation by (δμ)²/2σ, an effect of second order that a first-order estimate drops.
- The bounds do not drop it: they propagate μ's error into each deviation forward, without the cancellation that makes the cotangent zero. `cond` bounds the proposal's gap at 3·10⁻² (1.16 against 4.3·10⁻², `summary.md`'s column "proposal: `cond`").

**The solves:**

| budget (absolute) | entry | proposal: estimate | proposal: measured | rounds, in order | final gap | cost vs all-`f64` | μ alone widened |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10⁻³ (1.2e-3) | `std_two_pass` | 4.84e-5 | **4.25e-2** | 5: z, (z − μ)², the sum, the square root, each `f32`→`f64`, then **μ `bf16`→`f32` last** | 4.29e-8 | 1.00× | 6.11e-6, 2.00× |
| 3·10⁻² (3.7e-2) | `std_two_pass` | 7.86e-3 | 4.33e-2 | 7, μ last | 4.29e-8 | 1.00× | 5.81e-3, 2.02× |
| 10⁻¹ (1.2e-1) | `std_one_pass` | 9.14e-2 | 1.79e-1 | 3: z², the square root, E[z²], each `f32`→`f64` | 6.04e-6 | 1.32× | — |
| 3·10⁻² (3.7e-2) | `std_one_pass` | 3.06e-2 | 3.13e-3 | 0 | 3.13e-3 | 1.96× | — |
| 10⁻³ (1.2e-3) | `std_one_pass` | 4.76e-5 | 6.04e-6 | 0 | 6.04e-6 | 1.32× | — |

**The textbook's verdict on the two formulas is inverted here.**

- **The one-pass formula.** Its cancellation is one the estimate sees: it charges the rounding of E[z²] at E[z²]'s magnitude, 10⁶, so it keeps E[z²] in `f64` at every budget below 10⁻¹.
- **Where the one-pass form widens.** It widens at 10⁻¹ alone. There the estimate, 9.1·10⁻², is of a mean row, while the budget is on the largest gap over the 64 rows, which is twice that, 1.8·10⁻¹.
- **The two-pass formula,** the numerically right one, has the stationary point and widens at every budget from 3·10⁻² to 10⁻⁶: 4 to 7 rounds, ending at 1.00× each time.

### A sum's own roundings: equal terms

| type | `k` | `normwise` | `cond` | `rss` | measured | `cond` ÷ measured | `rss` ÷ measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `clock` `f32` | 1.00e-1 | 1.00e-1 | 1.00e-1 | 1.59e-3 | 1.58e-2 | 6.4× | **0.10×** |
| `noisy` `f32` | 1.42e-1 | 1.00e-1 | 1.00e-1 | 1.59e-3 | 4.70e-4 | 213× | 3.4× |

**What happens.**
- The kernel rounds every partial sum, 4 095 times, in `f32`. Adding the same term each time, the roundings share their sign over long stretches, so the error grows as K, not as √K.
- The measured 1.58·10⁻² is 650 single roundings of the sum (u · 409.6 = 2.44·10⁻⁵), where √4 095 is 64.
- `rss`, which assumes the roundings independent, is 10× below the gap; `cond`, which charges K − 1 roundings, is 6.4× above it.
- A little noise in the terms (`noisy`) decorrelates the roundings: the gap falls 34-fold to 4.7·10⁻⁴, and `rss` is 3.4× above it.

The solver's estimate charges the stored sum one rounding:

| budget (absolute) | entry | proposal: estimate | proposal: measured | rounds | final gap | cost vs all-`f64` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10⁻⁵ (4.1e-3) | `clock` | 7.32e-5 | **1.58e-2** | 1: the sum `f32`→`f64` | 6.10e-6 | 2.00× |
| 10⁻⁵ (4.1e-3) | `noisy` | 1.15e-4 | 3.80e-4 | 0 | 3.80e-4 | 2.00× |
| 10⁻³ (4.1e-1) | `clock` | 7.32e-5 | 1.58e-2 | 0 | 1.58e-2 | 2.00× |

Here the culprit is also the largest term of the estimate, so the one round widens the right value, and the realization keeps its 2.00×. The final gap, 6.1·10⁻⁶, is the terms' own rounding to `f32` added 4 096 times exactly: 4 096 · (f32(0.1) − 0.1).

## The bounds on the refused proposals

`summary.md`'s column "proposal: `cond`, `rss`" checks each proposal the measurement refused under the four rules (`presk check --model all --measure` on the realization before any widening):

| proposal refused | measured | `cond` | `rss` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `attn_raw`, 10⁻³ | 3.72e-1 | 1.79e-5 | 2.77e-6 |
| `std_two_pass`, 10⁻³ | 4.25e-2 | 1.15 | 2.69e-1 |
| `std_one_pass`, 10⁻¹ | 1.79e-1 | 1.70 | 2.56e-1 |
| `clock`, 10⁻⁵ | 1.58e-2 | 1.00e-1 | 1.59e-3 |

**Which quantity misjudged each case:**
- *The two-pass deviation, the one-pass at 10⁻¹, the equal terms:* the sound bound `cond` lies above the measured gap and above the budget, so it would have refused these three proposals as well. It was the solver's first-order estimate that misjudged them, not the bound.
- *The overflow:* the bound fails too.

The solver does not verify by the bound because on a transformer the bound is vacuous (D39: a relative bound of 10²⁰ on a loss of 4.5): it would refuse everything there. The measurement is the check that is neither vacuous nor blind.

## The three programs

| program | the estimate proposes | the measurement says | widening lands on |
|---|---|---|---|
| unshifted softmax (`attn_raw`), budget 10⁻³ | `f32` for the row's four stored values, estimate 6.3·10⁻⁶ against 3.7·10⁻⁴ | 0.372, the whole output: the row's sum of `exp`, 3.78·10³⁸, past `f32`'s range | `f64` after 4 rounds, gap 1.4·10⁻¹⁵; the same by the primitive: no round, 2.00× |
| two-pass standard deviation (`std_two_pass`), 10⁻³ | `bf16` for the mean, whose sensitivity is zero, estimate 4.8·10⁻⁵ against 1.2·10⁻³ | 4.3·10⁻², a second-order effect | after 5 rounds, the mean in `f32`, gap 4.3·10⁻⁸ |
| a sum of 4 096 equal terms (`clock`), 10⁻⁵ | `f32` for the sum, estimate 7.3·10⁻⁵ against 4.1·10⁻³ | 1.6·10⁻², correlated roundings | the sum in `f64` after 1 round, gap 6.1·10⁻⁶, 2.00× kept |

## Why `rss` fails here, and when it does not

- **`clock`: correlated roundings.** `rss` fails because its hypothesis is false, and only there. The partial sums of a constant term round the same way over long stretches, so the errors add instead of cancelling as √K. The same terms with a little noise round independently, and `rss` is 3.4× above the gap again.
- **The overflow: no rule holds.** `rss` and the three bounds all fail, for a reason no hypothesis about the roundings concerns: the value has left its type's range.
- **The variance: independence held.** It held on both forms in `f32` (`rss` 2.3× and 6.8× above the gap). On the two-pass form in `bf16`, `rss` is 0.68× the gap, the second-order effect of the mean's rounding again.

## What the widening does with a misjudged value

**It reaches the culprit last.** The measurement decides, but the widening's order is the estimate's: it widens the value with the largest term first. Where the estimate misjudged a value, that value's term is small, so the widening walks every innocent value back to `f64` before it reaches the culprit, and it never narrows the innocent ones again.

- The unshifted softmax at 10⁻³ ends at 1.00×; the row's sum and its reader alone in `f64` hold the budget at 1.33×.
- The two-pass deviation at 10⁻³ ends at 1.00×; μ alone in `f32` holds it at 2.00× with a gap of 6.1·10⁻⁶.

The realization the solver returns is inside its budget every time. It is far from the cheapest one inside the budget.

## Found and left

- **The range is unchecked.** Neither the bounds nor the estimate compare a value's envelope magnitude with its type's largest finite value. A type that cannot hold the envelope's magnitude could be refused before the measurement, or the bound printed as ∞. The sound rule `cond` is below the gap on `attn_raw` in `f32`, which its first-order statement does not cover and does not say.
- **The widening order is the estimate's.** Widening by the measured effect would find the culprit (one measurement per narrowed value per round, cheap on these programs). So would narrowing again after a widening holds.
- **∞ means two things.** A measured gap of ∞ means either that the run refused the realization or that its outputs were not finite (NaN reads as ∞, `gap::leaf_gap`). The solver's log does not tell the two apart. The defect fixed above hid behind this.
