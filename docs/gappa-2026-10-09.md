package), `make gappa` (`experiments/gappa.sh`, `experiments/gappa_layernorm.py`,

## 1. What was compared

Three numbers on the **same** nest, realization, order and hypotheses:

- **The measured gap**: `presk check --precision ENV --realize f32 --measure`,
  the largest difference between the native `f32` kernels' output and the
  exact reference at 128 bits, on the envelope's own inputs.
- **The compiler's bound**: the same command's `cond` (the default rule),
  with `k`, `normwise` and the `rss` estimate beside it (`--model all`),
  conditional on the envelope.
- **Gappa's proved bound**: `|ŷ₀ − y₀|` for the first element of the row,
  where `y₀` is the exact value and `ŷ₀` the same tree with one rounding
  operator per operation the kernels perform, in their order, from
  hypotheses that are the envelope's own magnitudes.

The nest is `LayerNorm{D}.apply` of `tests/examples/layernorm/layernorm.psk`
(the examples' listing), in `f32`, at `D = 8, 16, 32, 64`, `batch = 8`
rows synthesized from seed 0, `eps = 1e-5`, `γ = 1`, `β = 0`
(initialization). The widths 8 and 64 are the prompt's (the conformance
entry; the course GPT's width); 16 and 32 show the trend and Gappa's
cost. `D = 384` was not run: Gappa's time grows about sixfold per doubling
(4 s, 13 s, 86 s, 10 min) and the course GPT's width would take hours.

**Behind a wrapper.** The entry run is `Norm.apply` of
`experiments/gappa_layernorm.psk`, a module with no dimension argument whose
one function calls `LayerNorm{D}.apply` with `D` a `const` of the file,
because of a defect found at the first command (§5, finding 1): `presk check
--precision` on the example's own entry `LayerNorm{8}.apply` does not bind
the module's `{8}`, the mean's length falls back to 1 024 terms and the
bound printed is not its rule's. The wrapper's kernels are the example's
byte for byte (`experiments/gappa.sh` compares the two dumps at each
width), and its measured gap is the same number.

**The sample.** `--dim batch=8`: eight rows. `--samples 8`, the prompt's,
draws nothing without `--data` (§5, finding 2), so the rows are the sample;
the envelope's magnitudes, floors and sums are over the eight rows and the
measured gap is the largest over their 8·D elements. §3 also gives one row
(`batch = 1`), where every scalar site's floor is its ceiling.

## 2. The kernels and the Gappa file

`presk dump --stage kernel` on the entry in `f32` (`docs/gappa/kernel_D8.txt`;
the same shape at every width): four kernels, five launches,

```
mean axis model     s0 = 0; for i1 in 0..D { s0 = s0 + x[i0, i1] }; mu[i0] = s0 / D
fused 2 steps       q[i0, i1] = (x[i0, i1] - mu[i0]) * (x[i0, i1] - mu[i0])
mean axis model     s0 = 0; for i1 in 0..D { s0 = s0 + q[i0, i1] }; v[i0] = s0 / D
fused 2 steps       r[i0] = sqrt(v[i0] + eps)
fused 4 steps       y[i0, i1] = (((x[i0, i1] - mu[i0]) / r[i0]) * g[i1]) + b[i1]
```

So: the sums are sequential (`c = D − 1` roundings, the realization's
default order), a mean sums then divides by `D` (exact in binary when `D`
is a power of two), `real(eps)` is the `f32` nearest to the binary64 the
literal denotes (an `f32[]` input of the third kernel; the host converts
it), `x − μ` is computed twice to the same value, and `γ` and `β` are two
roundings, a product then a sum, not an `fma`. The `realize` dump
(`realize_D8.txt`): three fusion groups, `%5 = sub;pow 2`, `%10 =
add;sqrt`, `%15 = sub;div;mul;add`, storage and accumulation `f32`.

`experiments/gappa_layernorm.py ENV.json --width D [--realize f32]
[--hypotheses sites|input] [--prove BOUND] [--all-rows] -o FILE.g` writes
that tree twice: over the reals (`sx = (((0 + x0) + x1) + …)`, `mu = sx / D`,
`d_i = x_i − mu`, `q_i = d_i · d_i`, `v`, `ve = v + eps`, `r = sqrt(ve)`,
`z_i = d_i / r`, `w_i = z_i · g_i`, `y_i = w_i + b_i`, with `eps =
float<ieee_64,ne>(1e-5)`), and rounded (`X_i = rnd(x_i)`, `G_i`, `B_i`,
`E = rnd(eps)` — the inputs rounded once as they cross the seam, as every
backend does and as the compiler counts them — then `rnd` on every node in
the kernels' order). A sum's parenthesization does not change its real
value, and Gappa pairs each rounded node with its exact twin, so the real
tree mirrors the kernel's. The goal is `|Y0 − y0| in ?`; Gappa answers
with an exact dyadic, and the script run again with `--prove` that dyadic
writes `|Y0 − y0| <= M·2^e` as the goal: a proof Gappa checks, its exit
code the verdict (`*.proof.g`, `*.proof.out`). At `D = 8` every element of
the row is a goal (`--all-rows`): the hypotheses are symmetric in the
elements but the sequential sum is not, and the bounds differ by 0.3 %
(`9.149e-5` to `9.173e-5`); the largest is the bound reported.

**Hypotheses** (from the envelope's JSON; a ceiling widened by one part
in 10¹², a floor narrowed by as much):

- `sites` (the compiler's own conditions): `|x_i| ≤
  max|x − μ| + max|μ|` (the entry's arguments are not sites of the
  envelope, §5 finding 3, so the input's range is derived from two that
  are), `|g_i| ≤ 1`, `|b_i| ≤ 0`, and every site of the body as the exact
  run recorded it: `|μ|`, `|x − μ|`, `(x − μ)² ∈ [0, max]`, `σ² ∈ [min,
  max]`, `σ² + eps ∈ [min, max]`, `√ ∈ [min, max]`, `|x̂|`, `|x̂ γ|`,
  `|y|` — the floors where the compiler's quotient and square-root rules
  read one (`propagate.rs`, `floor_of`).
- `input`: `|x_i|`, `|g_i|`, `|b_i|` alone, what a user of Gappa without
  the envelope would write.

**Hints** (three, each with its reason in the file): `0 + X0 -> X0` and
`0 + Q0 -> Q0`, the sum's first addition being exact where Gappa would
charge it a rounding; `MU - mu -> (SX - sx) / D + (MU - SX / D)` and the
same for `V`, since Gappa does not pair `rnd(a / D)` with `a / D` on its
own (it still charges the quotient a rounding, which for a power of two is
zero — a slack of one `u` on `μ` and `σ²`, sound); and the quotient by the
square root in absolute terms, `Z0 - z0 -> (Z0 - D0 / R) + ((D0 - d0) / R
+ d0 (r - R) / (R r))`, since `x − μ` has no floor (an element can sit at
the mean) and Gappa's own rule for a quotient needs the numerator's
relative error — without it Gappa answers `8.57`, the interval's width.
This is the form the compiler's quotient rule calls `abs` (`PrimOp::Div`:
`err(x) / lo + |x| · err(y) / lo²`). No bisection. Gappa warns four times
per goal that the hint's denominators are assumed non-zero when it checks
the rewriting (`R`, `r`, `R·r`): under either hypothesis set `R` and `r`
are above `√eps`.

**Two things Gappa needed** beyond the hints. Its answer to a query is an
exact dyadic `M·2^e` with a six-digit decimal beside it, rounded to
nearest; a proof whose goal is the decimal fails by a hair when the
rounding went down (it did, `bf16` at `D = 16`), so the script carries the
dyadic. And Gappa stops refining an enclosure when a step improves it by
less than 1 % (`-Echange-threshold`); with a goal to meet instead of a
query it stopped at 8.12e-6 where the query had reached 7.96e-6 (one row,
`D = 16`), so the proofs run with the threshold at 10⁻⁴, and reach the
query's bound everywhere.

## 4. What it says

**The three numbers at the width of record.** On the layer norm at `D = 64`
in `f32`, on one row, the native kernels are `2.4·10⁻⁷` from the exact
value; the compiler's `cond` bound is `1.3·10⁻⁵`, 53× that; Gappa, given
the same envelope's magnitudes as hypotheses and the kernels' order,
proves `3.3·10⁻⁵`, 134× the measurement and 2.5× the compiler's bound.
Over eight rows: `4.1·10⁻⁷`, `1.4·10⁻⁵` (35×), `6.9·10⁻⁵` (168×, 4.8× the
compiler's); at `D = 8` on eight rows `1.4·10⁻⁷`, `2.8·10⁻⁶` (19×),
`9.2·10⁻⁵` (637×, 33× the compiler's), and on one row 2.33e-6 against
2.38e-6, the proof the tighter. So the proved bound is **at** the
compiler's when the envelope is one row's (0.98–2.5×) and **above** it,
by 33× at `D = 8` and 4.8× at 64, when the envelope is a sample's.

**Where the two analyses agree and where they part** (the site table).
On the mean, the centering, the square and the variance's sum the two are
within a factor of two of each other, and of the same shape — the
compiler's `k` rule and Gappa agree to a few per cent on `μ`, `x − μ` and
`(x − μ)²`, since both charge one rounding per operation at the site's
magnitude; `cond` is tighter on the variance (2.7e-6 against Gappa's
5.2e-6) because its rule takes the squares' errors as proportional to the
squares. They part at the **square root** and the **quotient**. The
compiler's rules there are in relative terms at the site's maximum:
`err(√v) = |√v|ₘₐₓ · (err(v)/|v|ₘₐₓ) / 2`, `err(a/b) = |a/b|ₘₐₓ · (err(a)/|a|ₘₐₓ
+ err(b)/|b|ₘₐₓ)`, each the smaller of a relative and an absolute form —
and a relative error at the site's maximum assumes a value's error
proportional to its magnitude, so that the row with the smallest variance
carries the smallest error. Gappa, with no such assumption, must hold the
goal for every point of the hypotheses' box, among them a row whose
variance is at the envelope's floor (0.18 at `D = 8`) while its errors are
those of the row with the largest terms: `err(r) = err(v + eps) /
(2 √(v + eps)ₘᵢₙ)` is 6.3e-6 against the compiler's 1.05e-6, and the
quotient's `|x − μ|ₘₐₓ · err(r) / rₘᵢₙ²` is 9.1e-5 against 2.3e-6. The
sum of squares' error itself is 5.2e-6 in both views; what differs is
which denominator it is divided by.

**So the compiler's bound on a sample is not a theorem of the sample's
envelope.** The magnitudes the envelope records at every site over eight
rows do not, by themselves, imply the compiler's `cond` bound, nor `k`'s or
`normwise`'s on this nest: what can be proved from them, with the kernels'
exact order, is 33× higher at `D = 8` (on one row's envelope the two
agree, below). The gap is the proportionality the compiler's quotient and
square-root rules take as given — the same assumption the manual states
for `cond`'s sums ("sound if each element's error is proportional to its
magnitude"), present in the other rules' quotients too. The measurement
supports the assumption: `cond` is 19–35× the measured gap at every
width, and the soundness test holds on 24 combinations. A proof does not
have it. The manual's "sound, sound, sound, estimate" for `k`, `normwise`,
`cond`, `rss` is, more exactly: sound on every entry checked, first-order
under a proportionality assumption at the quotients and roots on all four,
and under it at the sums on `cond` as well.

**What the envelope buys.** From the inputs' ranges alone, Gappa proves
`3.2·10³` at `D = 8` and `4.5·10³` at 64 — vacuous, since nothing then keeps the
variance from 0 and the denominator from `√eps = 3.2·10⁻³`, where
`x̂` reaches `2.7 / 3.2·10⁻³ ≈ 850` and its error with it. The envelope's
floor on `σ² + eps` is what turns `3.2·10³` into `9.2·10⁻⁵`: `3.5·10⁷`.
That is the measurement the compiler's whole analysis rests on, stated as
a factor.

**The widths.** The compiler's `k` bound grows with `D` (5.0e-6, 1.7e-5,
4.2e-5, 1.0e-4: the sum's `D − 1` roundings), `cond` more slowly (2.8e-6
to 1.4e-5), the measured gap slowest (1.4e-7 to 4.1e-7). Gappa's proved
bound does not grow (9.2e-5, 6.0e-5, 6.4e-5, 6.94e-5): it is dominated by
the quotient's absolute term, `|x − μ|ₘₐₓ · err(r) / rₘᵢₙ²`, which depends
on the sample's floors and not on the sum's length. So proved/`cond`
falls from 33× to 4.8× over the four widths, and would cross below 1 at
a width the envelope's floors would have to be the same on, which is to
say: the compiler's bound and the proved one are different objects, and
their ratio is a property of the sample's spread as much as of the rules.

**`f64` and `bf16`.** In `f64` the picture is `f32`'s at 2⁻²⁹: proved 33×
`cond` at `D = 8`, 14× at 16. In `bf16` the proved bound is vacuous (866
at `D = 8`): with `u = 2⁻⁸` the error the sum of squares can accumulate
under the hypotheses exceeds the variance's floor, so the computed root
can reach `√eps` and the quotient's term blows up — while the compiler's
`cond` is 6.2× the measurement. On a `bf16` normaliser the compiler's
bound rests on the proportionality assumption and on nothing else a proof
can see.

**One row.** With the envelope of a single row (`batch = 1`), every scalar
site's floor is its ceiling — the row's own `μ`, `σ²` and `√` — and the
proof and the compiler's bound meet: 2.33e-6 against `cond`'s 2.38e-6 at
`D = 8` (0.98×, the proof the tighter), 1.9× at 16 and 32, 2.5× at 64,
both 24–53× the measured gap. The proportionality the compiler's quotient
and square-root rules assume costs nothing when the hypotheses are one
row's: there is one variance to divide by, and the error paired with it
is that row's. What the eight-row envelope adds is a box — the smallest
variance of one row with the largest terms of another — and the compiler's
bound on that box (2.78e-6 at `D = 8`, a sixth more than on one row) is
not what a proof on that box gives (9.17e-5, forty times more): the
compiler's bound on a sample is, in effect, the bound on its worst row
taken one site at a time, and the proof's is the bound on every row the
envelope admits. The one-row figure is the proof of this nest's bound on
one row; the eight-row figure is what the
envelope, as the compiler conditions on it, actually implies.

## 5. Findings about the compiler (reported, not fixed)

The prompt's rule: no compiler change; a defect found is recorded here
and left to a later plan unless the user says otherwise.

1. **`presk check --precision` and `presk solve` do not bind a module's
   dimension arguments.** `check_precision` (`presk-cli/src/main.rs`, the
   `resolve_entry` call) discards the dimensions the entry's module
   arguments bind (`let Ok((entry, _, _)) = resolve_entry(…)`) and builds
   the propagation's `dims` from the envelope's `--dim`s filtered by the
   entry's own; `solve` has the same pattern. On `LayerNorm{8}.apply`, `D`
   is unbound, `terms()` in `presk-precision/src/propagate.rs` cannot read
   the mean's length off the type and falls back to `DEFAULT_TERMS = 1024`,
   so each mean's roundings are charged 1 023 times instead of 7. Measured
   (`docs/gappa/check_example_D8.txt`, eight rows): `k` 5.4e-4 instead of
   5.0e-6, `cond` 1.5e-4 instead of 2.8e-6, the measured gap the same
   1.44e-7; at `D = 64`, `k` 1.5e-3 (`cond` 2.0e-4) instead of 1.0e-4 (`check_example_D64.txt`).
   The soundness test (`presk-precision/tests/soundness.rs`) binds the
   dimensions through `prepare_run` and gives the manual's table today
   (`LayerNorm{8}` `f32`: measured 1.44e-7, `k` 6.88e-6, 48×; `cond`
   18×) — so the table of "Is the bound any use?" stands; the CLI's number on
   a module entry does not. The entries of record (`loss`, `squad_loss`,
   the decoder's loss) are top-level functions whose dimensions are
   `--const`s and `--dim`s, bound: their bounds are unaffected. The fix is
   one line (keep `resolve_entry`'s dims and merge them), in both commands.
2. **`presk calibrate --precision --samples N` without `--data` draws
   nothing.** `calibrate_precision` records `samples` in the envelope and
   prints "over N samples", and the run is the one synthesized input of
   `--seed`; `--samples` folds in a batch only when `--data` supplies one
   (the manual's `cli` says as much, in `--data`'s row). A warning when
   `--samples` is given without `--data`, or a synthesized batch of `N`,
   would make the message true. Here the rows are the sample
   (`--dim batch=8`).
3. **The entry's arguments are not sites of the envelope.** `propagate_with`
   takes an argument's magnitude from `env.get(site).unwrap_or(1.0)`, and
   the envelope records only the body's lets, so an input's rounding is
   charged at `u · 1` whatever its range: here `|x|` reaches 2.7 at `D =
   8` and 3.6 at 64, so the input's rounding is undercharged by that
   factor (1.6e-7 against 6.0e-8 in `f32`). It did not make a bound fall
   below the measurement (the margin is 19×), but the premise "the
   arguments arrive rounded once into their storage type" is counted at a
   guessed scale. Recording the arguments' magnitudes in the envelope is
   the fix. (The Gappa file derives `|x| ≤ max|x − μ| + max|μ|` from two
   sites that are recorded, a sound consequence of the envelope.)
4. **The bound for `y₀` is not exactly the bound for `yⱼ`.** The prompt
   expected the goal for `ŷ₀` to be every element's by renaming; the
   hypotheses are symmetric in the elements and the sequential sum is not
   (an element's rounding enters `μ` at its position), and the eight
   bounds at `D = 8` spread by 0.3 %. The summary takes the largest. For
   the compiler, which charges a sum `γ_{D−1} · Σ|terms|` whatever the
   position, this is nothing new; for a proof it means one goal per
   element, or the largest.

## 6. What it does not say

- Nothing about the loss or a transformer block: §4's nine orders between
  `cond` and the gap on the course GPT are eight sums in series, not this
  nest; on the nest, `cond` is 19–35× and a proof 230–640× the gap, both
  "within a factor of tens or hundreds". What Gappa adds is a theorem; what
  it takes is the proportionality the compiler's rules assume.
- Nothing proved about the envelope itself: both bounds are conditional on
  it, and a row outside it is outside both.
- No Gappa export: the generator knows one nest (its thirteen lets, by
  their operations) and nothing else; a Gappa printer for every kernel is
  `presk-other-export-targets-2026-09-26.md`'s §2, a later plan.
- Nothing about the native backend's transcendental functions (none in a
  layer norm) nor about the kernels' bits: the native `f32` arithmetic is
  IEEE binary32 to nearest, `sqrt` and `/` the machine's, which Gappa's
  `float<ieee_32,ne>` is; a kernel that did otherwise would make the
